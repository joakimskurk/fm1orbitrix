ORBITRIX

An orbital groove engine for the M-VAVE FM-1

Every rhythm is an interval, and every interval a rhythm.

▶ Play it here Install on your FM-1 Source

In development. It runs here exactly as on the FM-1 (the same code, compiled to WebAssembly), and it builds for the FM-1 and passes its tests, but it has not yet been tested on a real FM-1.

Play it here

This is the firmware itself, the same C as on the FM-1, running on a web FM-1. Press Start. After the splash (PLAY skips it) KITTEN is waiting, a beat to start from: press PLAY. SEQ opens its steps on the keys, EDIT its sounds by name, and PRESETS turns to MILKY WAY, a lullaby, and the fourteen systems of the sky. Astralica is the sun: she bobs to it, sings along when you play, and naps when you stop.

Press Start: the firmware loads when you do.

The computer's keyboard, once you have clicked the FM-1: white keys Z…/ and Q…Y, black ones the row above. Space PLAY, Enter REC, Esc HOME, Shift held is SEL.
The knobs: drag up or down, scroll, or the arrow keys. SEL latches here (click to hold it): with it, the knobs turn the second row, and a key moves the sun.
What it keeps (its settings, your systems in U1–U8: SAVE, SAVE) stays in this browser. A MIDI keyboard plays it with velocity, and its clock runs the orrery.
On STEPS (SEQ) the white keys are the track's steps: a click goes rest, hit, accent. The black ones, as the screen draws them: the lower F# G# A# C# pick track A–D, D# is IDEA (a new pattern, as the sound wants one), the upper four mute A–D, then CLEAR and UNDO.
The keys play the focus, the chip at the top (K: the keys' own sound; A–D: that track's). On SOUND (EDIT) K1 picks its sound by name; REC writes what you play into the track.

The legend of Astralica

In the beginning the universe was silent. Then came Astralica. She grew, and grew: one big ass cat. She ate all the planets, and in her tummy they changed; and when she pooped them out, they formed the ORBITRIX. Now her song rings through the multiverses.

Seventeen seconds, drawn and scored by the firmware itself (its own screen and engine, filmed on a computer): a drone, a chirp as each planet falls in, her tummy glowing and gurgling, then plop, plop, plop, plop: four little cosmic swirls become the kittens that fly out to their rings, and each ring sings as its kitten arrives; last her meow, echoing from universe to universe. The kittens are the planets that go round her on the FM-1.

Little songs

Four of the systems, each arranged as a little song by the firmware's own engine: the chords alone in their halo (or the drone), then the parts come in one by one, a break where the keys sing the chords' own notes, all of it again, and the tail ringing out. The bass and the melodies FOLLOW the chords: they play the chord's own notes, so nothing can clash.

Hear the systems

Six of the twenty, as unlike each other as they come, each for a few bars with a slow progression on the keys, rendered by the firmware's own engine. The twenty: KITTEN and MILKY WAY to start from, the 4:5:6:7 idea, lo-fi, house, disco, a generative spiral, bossa nova, dub, chiptune, a music box in twelve-eight, synth pop, a gamelan, a lullaby, ambient, techno, and four from the legend's own universe (ASTRALICA, NOM NOM, TUMMY, MULTIVERSE). The picture is the spectrum, low notes at the bottom: click it to jump.

How it works

Orbit synthesis

The voice is a planet on a Kepler ellipse, and you hear where it is. ECC brightens it (its spectrum is Bessel functions, as FM's), SLING hears its speed, L and R are two views of one orbit, a moon adds harmonics or bells.

Gravity

Everything is tuned to the sun: just and overtone scales, keys retuned to their ratio, or the sun's harmonics themselves. Above 50 % a note falls into tune on a spring, overshooting and settling.

The orrery

Four rings step at ratios to a 16th (1:1, 5:4, 3:2, 7:4 …) on a clock that makes them meet exactly. Where they meet, the hits are accented. Logic between them (AND, NOT, XOR, CONJ), mutation, spirals.

A groove in a minute

Pick a track with a black key, tap its steps on the white ones, or ask for an IDEA: a kick on the beat, a snare's backbeat, hats between, a bass on the root, a melody on the chord's tones. UNDO takes it back.

Sounds by name

Thirty, from BOOM, PAT and TICK to BOOP, SQUEAK, PURR, PAW, TOY, STAR, MEW and COMET, each balanced and in its own register; TONE, DECAY and LEVEL under them, and the whole orbit beneath that.

The strange part

TIDE: the planets pull on each other (FM between the voices, on the sun's harmonics). INSPIRAL: merger chirps. BEAM: pulsar pulses. HORIZON: the music falls into a black hole, redshifted, frozen, partly backwards. And under it all the background radiation: chords in the tuning's own notes, voice-led, never gliding, all wet in a halo of their own; TIRED LIGHT: echoes that come back a just interval redder each time.

Install

  1. Open this page in Chrome or Edge on a computer (they can talk to the FM-1 over USB MIDI).
  2. Connect the FM-1 directly to the computer with a USB data cable, and switch it on.
  3. Close other apps that use MIDI, tick the box, press Install and allow MIDI when the browser asks.
